01 Ya Qalbi

Lebanese Arabic, the way it’s spoken at home.

A language app that teaches Lebanese Arabic the way families actually speak it.

Role
Website design, mobile app redesign
Team
Chayland Design
Platform
iOS app, marketing site
Status
Coming soon to the App Store
Live site
yaqalbiapp.com

A The brief

Most Arabic apps teach Modern Standard Arabic, the formal language of books and the news. Ya Qalbi teaches Lebanese: the words you hear from family, at the bakery, on the street in Beirut. The product needed to feel warm and personal, and the lessons had to fit into a few minutes a day.

B The challenge

Language apps tend to look alike. Ya Qalbi had to explain in one glance why it is different, and every lesson had to carry two scripts, Arabic and Latin letters, plus audio, without feeling crowded on a small screen.

C Key decisions

What I designed, and why.

  1. 01 Decision

    Lead with the difference

    The site opens on what makes Ya Qalbi worth switching to: “Real Lebanese, not textbook Arabic.” The copy names real situations, like greetings, ordering food and catching up with family, so the value is concrete.

  2. 02 Decision

    Companions, not mascots

    Six phoenixes, each from a different part of Lebanon, guide the learner through every lesson. Giving them names and places turns decoration into a reason to come back.

  3. 03 Decision

    Rewards with meaning

    Lessons earn dahab, Lebanese for gold. Instead of points with no purpose, dahab unlocks stories about Lebanon, so progress teaches culture as well as words.

✦ App redesign

One prompt per screen, always in reach.

Arabic script stays large with Latin letters underneath, audio is one tap away, and the main action sits in the thumb zone. Your phoenix and your dahab follow you through every lesson.

D On mobile

Designed for the phone first.

Most visitors arrive on a phone, so every section was designed to read and work at small sizes first.

E Outcome

What shipped

  • A website that explains the product in a single scroll and collects launch sign-ups.
  • An app redesign built around one prompt per screen, big Arabic script with Latin letters below, and audio always one tap away.
  • A character system the brand can keep growing across lessons, emails and social.

If I kept going

I’d test the onboarding with heritage learners and complete beginners separately. Their first five minutes need different things.
