03 Orlando’s Oasis

Pool care that explains itself.

Pool care that shows its price up front, plus a customer web app for every visit.

Role
Website design, web app team
Team
Chayland Design
Platform
Website, customer web app
Market
Palm Beach County, FL
Live site
orlandosoasispools.com

A The brief

Orlando’s Oasis is a residential pool service in the West Palm Beach area. Every plan includes chemicals, and every visit ends with photos and a report. The business needed a site that turns visitors into booked first visits, and a web app where customers manage everything after that.

B The challenge

Most pool companies hide pricing behind “call for a quote”. Homeowners want to know three things before they book: what it costs, what’s included, and who will be in their backyard. The site had to answer all three before the first scroll ends.

C Key decisions

What I designed, and why.

  1. 01 Decision

    Price on the homepage

    Right after the hero, a plan picker shows real prices by pool size, with a 12-month plan or month-to-month. Verified reviews sit next to it, so trust and price are read together.

  2. 02 Decision

    Three steps, no surprises

    Pick your plan, have your first visit, then ongoing care. The $75 first visit is credited to the first month, which removes the risk of trying.

  3. 03 Decision

    Proof over promises

    A before-and-after gallery of real customer pools does more than any headline. It shows the work instead of describing it.

  4. 04 Decision

    A portal for every visit

    I’m part of the team building the customer web app: booking, visit photos and reports, rescheduling, messaging the office, and managing plans, payments and invoices in one account.

D On mobile

Designed for the phone first.

Most visitors arrive on a phone, so every section was designed to read and work at small sizes first.

E Outcome

What shipped

  • Clear pricing and a single repeated action: “Book my first visit”.
  • Trust built from verified reviews, background-checked technicians and real photos.
  • A plain-language FAQ and fine print so customers know the terms before they sign.
  • A web app in progress that moves booking, reports and billing out of phone calls.

If I kept going

Next, I want to use visit-report data in the portal to show each customer their pool’s history over time.
